# Kestrel Racing M5 step 1: Track Kit v1 and Salt Flats

The first authored track. It is built in Blender by a script, exported by our add-on to the neutral `.ktrk.json`, and turned
into the cart's binary by the converter. Everything after the export runs without Blender, so the gate needs none.
PORT_PLAN §3.3-3.5 is the design; this is what was built.

```
tools/racer/tracks/salt_flats_build.py --(Blender 5.3)--> scene --(add-on)--> salt_flats.ktrk.json   (committed)
salt_flats.ktrk.json --(trackc.py -> kit.py: budget check, validation, baked colours)--> apps/zig/assets/screens/racer/salt.ktrk
salt.ktrk --(walk.py: the priced-frame walk)--> salt_flats.heavy.json (the 5 heaviest flyby frames, pinned by racer_salt.mjs)
```

## Authoring conventions (freds72's, PORT_PLAN §3.5, rewritten for Blender 5.3 and our format)

- **Vertex groups.** A face belongs to a group when all of its vertices do. The first match in this order wins:
  - `DETAIL_FACE`: a decal, attached to the ground face under it (coplanar, inside its edges within 0.02 units);
  - `TRACK_FACE`: asphalt, drivable;
  - `GROUND_FACE`: drivable off-road;
  - `PROP_FACE`: scenery;
  - anything else is a wall.
- **Borders.** `BORDER1..3` (any `BORDER*` group): an edge of a ground face whose two vertices share one is a wall edge for the physics.
- **The racing line.**
  - `TRACK_L` / `TRACK_R` are the left and right edge loops.
  - `LINE_START` holds segment 0's L vertex. The walk goes the way grid slot 0 faces, and each L vertex takes the one R vertex joined to it by an edge.
  - `CHECKPT_1..n` each hold one L vertex, in lap order. The checkpoint is the segment that ends there, so the start line is the last one.
- **The start grid.** The 8 objects parented to an object named `start_grid`, sorted by name, facing their local +Y.
- **Materials.**
  - The custom property `ktrk_rgb = [r, g, b]` is the palette colour of a face square to the sun.
  - `ktrk_dual = 1` makes a material double-sided.
  - The material name is the ramp name.
- **Scene properties.**
  - `ktrk_name`.
  - `ktrk_mood` (a name).
  - `ktrk_sun`, a vector in Blender axes.
  - `ktrk_far_ground`, the material the ground beyond the draw distance shows. It goes on ramp 1, as on every track.
- **Axes.** Blender is Z-up and the engine is y-up. The exporter maps (x, y, z) to (x, z, y), a reflection.
  - A face wound counter-clockwise seen from outside keeps its outward normal under the engine's `(last - first) x (second - first)`.
  - Every non-ground face is checked against Blender's normal, so a face wound inside out is refused, not culled from the wrong side.

## The budget check (`limits.py`; the meter shows the same rows)

| limit | value | kind | where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| colour ramps | 23 | HARD | 16 + 30 ramps x 8 levels = 256 entries; ramps 12-14 are the player's car, 15-18 the AI liveries |
| racing-line segments | 256 | HARD | `seg.MAXSEG`, `minimap.MAXPTS` |
| start grid | 8 slots | HARD | the race |
| checkpoints | strictly ascending | HARD | `trk.check` refuses the track at init otherwise |
| a face with no area | refused | HARD | it has no normal (the validator divided by zero on one) |
| faces in the busiest tile (with their decals) | 64 | SOFT | our budget; the stress city (100 a tile) is the over-budget test case and is not checked |
| faces (with decals) | 3,000 | SOFT | our budget |
| vertices | 8,192 | SOFT | our budget (the vertex cache is sized to the largest track; u16 ids allow 65,535) |
| the priced frame at R = 5 | 1.55 Mc | SOFT | `walk.py`, PORT_PLAN §2.3's low column plus FRESCO's plane; not re-fitted to the board yet |

A refusal names the limit, its kind and the busiest tile, e.g. `SOFT limit: faces in the busiest tile 65 > 64 (tile 23,23)`.
Every validation error has a unit test: `test_racer_kit.py` covers the budget and the palette, and `test_racer_kit_export.py` covers the conventions and `ktrk_check`'s remaining errors.
- The planted over-budget tile is 65 small props on tile (23, 23).
- `ktrk_check`'s "border faces outward" is the exception: it cannot be reached without the face also being wound against the inside test, which M0 already tests.

## Baked face colours (step 0's design decision)

The arcade Virtua Racing look: each face stores its own colour, neighbouring faces sit in close tones, there is no lighting at run
time and no dithering anywhere. `bake.py` bakes the tone at convert time, from the face normal against the track's fixed sun, onto the
palette levels of the face's ramp:

    level = 4 + round(3 * max(0, n . sun))        (palette.zig lit: level 7 = 99 %, one level = 23/256 of the channel)

- A face square to the sun shows its authored colour.
- A face turned away is at most 3 levels (~27 %) darker.
- On a flat lake every ground normal is the same, so **the road panels' alternation is authored**: two close materials, `asphalt_a` / `asphalt_b`, alternating every two segments.

Measured (`test_racer_kit.py` pins the gap at 10-25 luma, mid tones):

| Salt Flats, noon (sun = (0.3, 1.0, -0.4) engine axes) | level | shown RGB | luma |
|---|---|---|---|
| asphalt_a (panel) | 7 | 104, 104, 108 | 104.5 |
| asphalt_b (panel) | 7 | 90, 90, 96 | 90.7 |
| salt (far ground, ramp 1) | 7 | 169, 164, 148 | 164 |
| shoulder | 7 | 148, 140, 122 | 141 |
| barrier wall facing the sun / away | 5 / 4 | | about 18 / 27 % darker |

The far ground (palette index 4) takes the salt ramp at the flat level 7 (`trackset.zig` `SALT_FLAT_LEVEL`, pinned by a test), so no
seam shows where the draw distance ends. **Key `i`** works here as on the VR tracks:
- FLAT (the default) is the baked colour;
- LIT is the converter's old light by the normal;
- SOFT is a gentle light (levels 5-7).

The record's `alt` word carries LIT and SOFT. The far ground drops to level 4 in LIT and SOFT. The oval and the city are untouched: their bytes are pinned as before.

## Salt Flats

Original content (ours): a dry-lake speedway at noon.
- **Course.** A superellipse loop, about 200 x 212 units, with one sweeping bay pulled in on the far side; 176 segments of ~4.3 units.
- **Surfaces.** The road is 12 units wide, between 3-unit compacted-salt shoulders and low barriers (0.5 high, alternating red and white every 3 segments).
- **Decals.** Centre dashes, kerbs on the inside of every turn sharper than the straights, a start line.
- **Props.** A grandstand on the start straight's outside, a timing tower in the infield, 11 pylons, three low buttes on the lake.
- **Not here yet.** The heat-haze horizon is left for M6, and the sky and the backdrop panorama are still the shared ones (green hills: a desert panorama is content for later).

| | Salt Flats | oval |
|---|---|---|
| vertices | 1,649 | 1,301 |
| faces (+ decals) | 949 (+223) = 1,172 | 600 (+301) |
| busiest tile | 19 faces | |

**In the cart.**
- **The track set.** `t` cycles oval, city, salt in the plain cart. The `-Dracer_vr` cart is unchanged (oval + VR1-3), because its RAM window is the VR tracks'. That cart was not rebuilt by this step.
- **Racing.** On Salt Flats the race (`e`, `x`) runs with the player on the AI's pilot, as on the VR tracks: the recorded button streams are the oval's. `b` (lap v1) goes back to the oval.
- **Pins.** The oval, city, drive, race and VR pins are unchanged.

**The gate** (`racer_salt.mjs`, 3 lines):
- the 8 attract poses (A-H, R = 5) are pinned, and CPU = AFFINE byte for byte;
- the walk's 5 heaviest flyby poses (frames 234, 90, 123, 192, 279) are rendered by the Python reference renderer (`ref_render.py --frame`) with **pixel diff 0** against the cart;
- the flyby's 12-entry CRC list is pinned and the same on both fills;
- key `i` changes the view and gives it back;
- the race benchmark runs on Salt Flats;
- the far ground (palette index 4) is the colour of a baked flat salt face (no seam at the draw distance);
- breaks: `--break pin` and `--break ref` (the reference asked for the next frame) are each caught.

## Frame cost (estimates)

- **The converter's walk** (PORT_PLAN §2.3 low column, AFFINE pixels): the heaviest flyby pose at R = 5 is 0.82 Mc (85 polygons, 665 spans). At R = 8 it is 0.83 Mc, because the lake is empty beyond the road. The oval prices at 0.84 Mc the same way.
- **`frame_cost`** (wasm op counts, the plain multiply twin; `node apps/harness/racer_vr_costs.mjs --tracks 2` on the plain cart reaches Salt Flats with two taps of `t`, the label says VR2):

| scene | R | CPU fill | AFFINE fill |
|---|---|---|---|
| heaviest attract pose B (flyby, = heavy frame 90) | 5 | 1.14 Mc, ~60 fps | 1.18 Mc, ~60 fps |
| the same | 8 | 1.19 Mc, ~60 fps | 1.24 Mc, ~60 fps |
| race grid, 8 cars (lap v2 start, pilot) | 5 | 2.07 Mc, ~48 fps | 2.02 Mc, ~50 fps |
| race mid-lap (AI ticking) | 5 | 3.34 Mc, ~30 fps | 3.19 Mc, ~31 fps |

The model's I$ term is ~2x pessimistic on this cart: it said 3.06 Mc for the oval's mid-lap and the board ran 60 fps (M3.md). Halved, Salt Flats'
race is ~1.6-1.7 Mc, at the 60 fps edge like the oval's, and the flyby has a wide margin. **Proposed R: 8** (the play default) for
driving and the flyby, and the bench laps at 5 as everywhere. The board decides.
