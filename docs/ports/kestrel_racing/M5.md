The first authored track. It is built in Blender by a script, exported by our add-on to the neutral `.ktrk.json`, and turned
into the cart's binary by the converter. Everything after the export runs without Blender, so the gate needs none.
PORT_PLAN §3.3-3.5 is the design; this is what was built.

```

salt_flats.ktrk.json --(trackc.py -> kit.py: budget check, validation, baked colours)--> apps/zig/assets/screens/racer/salt.ktrk
salt.ktrk --(walk.py: the priced-frame walk)--> salt_flats.heavy.json (the 5 heaviest flyby frames, pinned by racer_tracks.mjs)
```

## Authoring conventions (freds72's, PORT_PLAN §3.5, rewritten for Blender 5.3 and our format)

- **Vertex groups.** A face belongs to a group when all of its vertices do. The first match in this order wins:
  - `DETAIL_FACE`: a decal, attached to the ground face under it (coplanar, inside its edges within 0.02 units);
  - `TRACK_FACE`: asphalt, drivable;
  - `GROUND_FACE`: drivable off-road;
  - `PROP_FACE`: scenery;
  - anything else is a wall.
- **Borders.** `BORDER1..3` (any `BORDER*` group): an edge of a ground face whose two vertices share one is a wall edge for the physics.
- **The racing line.**
  - `TRACK_L` / `TRACK_R` are the left and right edge loops.
  - `LINE_START` holds segment 0's L vertex. The walk goes the way grid slot 0 faces, and each L vertex takes the one R vertex joined to it by an edge.
  - `CHECKPT_1..n` each hold one L vertex, in lap order. The checkpoint is the segment that ends there, so the start line is the last one.
- **The start grid.** The 8 objects parented to an object named `start_grid`, sorted by name, facing their local +Y.
- **Materials.**
  - The custom property `ktrk_rgb = [r, g, b]` is the palette colour of a face square to the sun.
  - `ktrk_dual = 1` makes a material double-sided.
  - The material name is the ramp name.
- **Scene properties.**
  - `ktrk_name`.
  - `ktrk_mood` (a name).
  - `ktrk_sun`, a vector in Blender axes.
  - `ktrk_far_ground`, the material the ground beyond the draw distance shows. It goes on ramp 1, as on every track.
- **Axes.** Blender is Z-up and the engine is y-up. The exporter maps (x, y, z) to (x, z, y), a reflection.
  - A face wound counter-clockwise seen from outside keeps its outward normal under the engine's `(last - first) x (second - first)`.
  - Every non-ground face is checked against Blender's normal, so a face wound inside out is refused, not culled from the wrong side.

## The budget check (`limits.py`; the meter shows the same rows)

| limit | value | kind | where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| colour ramps | 23 | HARD | 16 + 30 ramps x 8 levels = 256 entries; ramps 12-14 are the player's car, 15-18 the AI liveries |
| racing-line segments | 256 | HARD | `seg.MAXSEG`, `minimap.MAXPTS` |
| start grid | 8 slots | HARD | the race |
| checkpoints | strictly ascending | HARD | `trk.check` refuses the track at init otherwise |
| a face with no area | refused | HARD | it has no normal (the validator divided by zero on one) |
| faces in the busiest tile (with their decals) | 64 | SOFT | our budget; the stress city (100 a tile) is the over-budget test case and is not checked |
| faces (with decals) | 3,000 | SOFT | our budget |
| vertices | 8,192 | SOFT | our budget (the vertex cache is sized to the largest track; u16 ids allow 65,535) |
| the priced frame at R = 5 | 1.55 Mc | SOFT | `walk.py`, PORT_PLAN §2.3's low column plus FRESCO's plane; not re-fitted to the board yet |

A refusal names the limit, its kind and the busiest tile, e.g. `SOFT limit: faces in the busiest tile 65 > 64 (tile 23,23)`.
Every validation error has a unit test: `test_racer_kit.py` covers the budget and the palette, and `test_racer_kit_export.py` covers the conventions and `ktrk_check`'s remaining errors.
- The planted over-budget tile is 65 small props on tile (23, 23).
- `ktrk_check`'s "border faces outward" is the exception: it cannot be reached without the face also being wound against the inside test, which M0 already tests.

## Baked face colours (step 0's design decision)

The arcade Virtua Racing look: each face stores its own colour, neighbouring faces sit in close tones, there is no lighting at run
time and no dithering anywhere. `bake.py` bakes the tone at convert time, from the face normal against the track's fixed sun, onto the
palette levels of the face's ramp:

    level = 4 + round(3 * max(0, n . sun))        (palette.zig lit: level 7 = 99 %, one level = 23/256 of the channel)

- A face square to the sun shows its authored colour.
- A face turned away is at most 3 levels (~27 %) darker.
- On a flat lake every ground normal is the same, so **the road panels' alternation is authored**: two close materials, `asphalt_a` / `asphalt_b`, alternating every two segments.

Measured (`test_racer_kit.py` pins the gap at 10-25 luma, mid tones):

| Salt Flats, noon (sun = (0.3, 1.0, -0.4) engine axes) | level | shown RGB | luma |
|---|---|---|---|
| asphalt_a (panel) | 7 | 104, 104, 108 | 104.5 |
| asphalt_b (panel) | 7 | 90, 90, 96 | 90.7 |
| salt (far ground, ramp 1) | 7 | 169, 164, 148 | 164 |
| shoulder | 7 | 148, 140, 122 | 141 |
| barrier wall facing the sun / away | 5 / 4 | | about 18 / 27 % darker |

The far ground (palette index 4) takes the salt ramp at the flat level 7 (since step 2: the file's mood block, pinned by a test), so no
seam shows where the draw distance ends. **Key `i`** works here as on the VR tracks:
- FLAT (the default) is the baked colour;
- LIT is the converter's old light by the normal;
- SOFT is a gentle light (levels 5-7).

The record's `alt` word carries LIT and SOFT. The far ground drops to level 4 in LIT and SOFT. The oval and the city are untouched: their bytes are pinned as before.

# M5 step 2: tracks off the disk, Neon Docks and Glacier Pass

## Tracks off the disk (`racer/library.zig`, `racer/trackset.zig`)

Matt approved it ("even better so people will use their own tracks"): with oval + city + Salt Flats built in, the 2 MiB cart
window had 172 KB free and every new track is ~0.4-0.7 MB. Now:

- **The cart embeds only the oval.** It is the fallback when a file is refused or the disk is absent (the gate's harnesses
  without a disk), and the recorded button streams (lap v1, lap v2's player) are the oval's. The cart's wasm went from 1.62 MB
  to 0.64 MB.
- **Every other track is a file**, `NAME.KTR` (the converter's v3 binary, raw or ZX0-packed):
  - on the cart's own `.zmd` disk: `tools/mkdisks.sh` packs CITY, SALT, NEON and GLACIER (`zx0pack`, cached like the carts;
    the disk is 823 KB). The cart lists them from the disk's FAT (`zg.disk`) in the FAT's order;
  - in **`C:\RACER\`** (the SD card's `/HD/RACER/`, through KOS GEMDOS read-only: `@import("kos_gemdos")`, the existing ABI,
    system table 1.8; nothing new was needed). These are listed after the disk's.
- **One buffer.** At init the library reads each file's first block (a v3 header whose total is the file's size, or a ZX0
  header's stated length), then allocates one buffer for the largest track (738 KB: the city) and one for the largest packed
  file (238 KB). A file that does not fit what the RAM window has left is dropped from the list by name. Cart RAM free after
  that: **158 KB** (`racer_tracks.mjs` requires >= 64).
- **Key `t`** walks the list: OVAL, CITY, SALT, NEON, GLACIER (then C:'s). A load first moves driving, the race and the minimap
  back to the oval (the buffer is about to change under them), reads the file (packed: into the stage buffer, then `zx0.depack`
  into the track buffer), parses it, and rebinds them. The frame's vertex cache and face stamps are sized to fixed caps
  (`library.MAX_VERTS` 25,600 and `MAX_FACES` 10,240, the city's size), so a track over them is refused, not overrun.
- **Every file is validated, and a bad one is refused, never trapped on.** `trk.parse` now treats the header as untrusted input
  (it is outside the CRC):
  - block bounds in u64, so a count cannot wrap;
  - at most 30 ramps (the 256-entry palette), 256 racing-line segments, 1-8 grid slots, an R_BAKE of 8;
  - every PVS list inside its block, and the mood block checked;
  - native tests corrupt each header word.
  A refused file logs `racer,track,refused,<NAME>,<why>` (BadPack, BadCrc, BadBlock, BadMood, NoGrid, TooBig...), is marked bad
  and skipped by `t` from then on, and the oval is shown. A load logs `racer,load,<NAME>,<stored>,<raw>,<disk blocks>,<us>`.
- **The mood block** (`tools/racer/mood.py`; header word 25 = its offset, 0 = none; the file's last 32 bytes, inside the CRC)
  replaces what the cart knew by track index:
  - the far ground's ramp and light level (the city's plaza ramp 8; Salt Flats' baked flat level 7; the VR tracks' level 7);
  - a BENCH_ONLY flag (the city: drive and race modes go back to the oval);
  - the sky's top and horizon colours (the copper's per-line gradient);
  - the backdrop's three colours (palette 12-14) and its shape: `hills` (M1's strip), `docks` or `peaks`, built once at a track
    change by integer maths in `backdrop.zig` and drawn the same by `tools/racer/ref_sky.py`.

  The oval has no block (its bytes are unchanged). The city and Salt Flats gained one: their files are their old bytes plus
  32 bytes and a header word, so **every oval, city, Salt Flats, drive and race pin is unchanged**.
- **The Track Kit scene properties** `ktrk_sky` (2 x RGB), `ktrk_hills` (3 x RGB) and `ktrk_backdrop` set the look. They are
  optional (the oval's look where absent), and the export carries them in `mood`.

### How a user adds a track

1. Build it in Blender with the add-on (above), export the `.ktrk.json`.
2. `python3 -I tools/racer/trackc.py my.ktrk.json -o MYTRACK.KTR`: the budget check, then the binary. Raw is fine; ZX0
   (`zig-out/bin/zx0pack MYTRACK.KTR packed.KTR`) loads faster.
3. Copy it to the SD card's `/HD/RACER/` (C:\RACER\ for the cart; 8.3 name, `.KTR`). Press `t` until its name shows.

In the browser the page's `?c=` image is C:. `racer_library.mjs` builds a FAT image with `\HD\RACER\MYTRACK.KTR` (Salt Flats'
raw binary) and a `NOTES.KTR` that is not a track. The first is listed after the disk's tracks and flown; the second is refused
at the scan.

### Load times

| track | on the disk (ZX0) | raw | disk blocks | node (load + depack + parse) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CITY | 228 KB | 756 KB | 447 | 15 ms |
| SALT | 104 KB | 383 KB | 203 | 6 ms |
| NEON | 128 KB | 430 KB | 250 | 7 ms |
| GLACIER | 243 KB | 732 KB | 476 | 13 ms |

**Board estimate (not measured).** On the board ZWINDOW leaves the disk in DDR and ZIGGY serves `diskReadBlock` by copying
from there (docs/hardware/FPGA_GLASS.md, "Disk drive"). A block is a 512-byte copy, ~1-2 kcycles, so the city's 447 blocks
take ~0.5-1 Mc. Bad Apple's README has no measured block-read figure (its README calls the board's disk read "the unknown"),
so this rests on the copy alone.

The rest is the CPU's:
- the depack, ~10 cycles a byte (Bad Apple's estimate): 7.5 Mc for the city;
- the CRC and the checks, the same order again.

That makes **~0.15-0.2 s for the city or Glacier Pass, ~0.1 s for Salt Flats**: a pause at the track change, never in a
lap. A C: file costs ~0.5 ms a 32 KB request on top (KOS_GEMDOS.md): ~6 ms for a raw 383 KB track. The board's log line
gives the real microseconds (board to-do 1).

## The car shadow (shadow.zig), key `y`

Matt's ask. One quad a car, sorted after the ground under it and before the car's faces, and drawn by darkening what the plane already holds:

- **REAL (the default).** Each pixel drops one level down its ramp.
  - The plane holds `16 + ramp * 8 + level`, so one level down is index - 1.
  - A ramp's bottom (level 0) stays: never into the ramp below. The first 16 entries (sky, HUD, far ground, backdrop) are left alone.
  - It runs four pixels a word (SWAR: the "level != 0" and "index >= 16" tests on each byte at once, with no borrow between bytes). The unaligned head and tail go a byte at a time, because a misaligned word traps on the board.
- **FAKE (key `y`).** The arcade's checkerboard: every pixel with (x + y) even becomes DITHER (index 0, black), and the others are untouched. Nothing is read.
  - The bench HUD line shows `/DITHER` after the track name.
  - The console logs `racer,shadow,REAL|DITHER`.
- **The AFFINE fill.** A shadow span reads the plane, so the sink fires what is queued and waits for CHISEL (`blitSync`) once per run of shadow spans (one car). Then the CPU darkens the span as the CPU path does.

**Cost (estimates).**
- **The CPU path:** ~1.5 cycles a pixel in the word loop, plus ~30 a span. A near car's shadow is ~100-300 pixels and a far one a few, so ~2-5 kcycles a frame for 8 cars. The model shows no difference between REAL and FAKE (2.62 / 2.62 Mc on the oval grid).
- **The AFFINE path** adds a flush and a sync for each visible car: ~800 cycles of op overhead plus the stall, up to ~10 kcycles at the grid.
- **The shadow's face lookup** (`nearCorner`: one `ground.findFace`, the car's own face tried first) is ~1-2 kcycles a car.

**Frame cost with the GT car and its shadow** (`frame_cost`, plain multiply twin, R = 5, lap v2 on the pilot; "before" is step 2):

| Salt Flats | 2.39 / 2.27 | 2.07 | 3.63 / 3.45 | 3.34 |

## Car files (`racer/carfile.zig`, `racer/cars.zig`), Track Kit spirit

When a track goes on show the cart looks for `<TRACK>.KCR` (that track's cars), then `CAR.KCR` (every track's), on its own
disk and then in `C:\RACER\` (a user's, as for tracks), and draws the first it finds; with none, the built-in GT.

- **The format** is carc.py's: v1 = one model (the GT is one: a car authored with the Car Kit works as a file as it is); v2 =
  a SET (`carc.pack_set`): a 64-byte header (magic, 2, 1-5 models, a flag, the seven car ramps 12-18 as RGB, a CRC-32) and the
  models' v1 records. Model 0 is the player's car, the AI cars take models 1.. in turn. A set's palette recolours ramps 12-18
  while it is on show (the livery recolour still applies to faces flagged body / stripe, which an imported car has none of).
- A face may carry its own colour (material `fixed`: a ramp 12-18 and a level 0-7, unlit): an imported car's colours.
- **Bounded parse, never a trap** (`carfile.parse`, like `trk.parse`): sizes, CRC, model count, vertices <= 256 (the car's
  vertex cache), faces <= 256 a model, part ends in order, a hull and a far model, ONE shadow quad (and no other face
  flagged shadow), every face 3-4 ids, all four in range. A refused file is named with its reason
  (`racer,car,refused,NAME,BadCrc`, and BAD CAR on the track select's refusals) and the next is tried (a bad `<TRACK>.KCR`
  falls back to `CAR.KCR`, then to the GT).
- **RAM**: one 32 KB buffer (the file depacked; a set's slices point into it); the file is staged in the track library's
  stage (a car is read after its track). The 32X sets are 20-24 KB raw. The local 13-track disk keeps the city's 738 KB
  buffer: 94 KB of cart RAM left (141 before the car buffer).
- `carcheck.py`: the HARD vertex limit is now 256 (the cart's cache; it was the u16 id), plus 256 faces; `fixed` colours checked.
- **Tests:** `carfile_test.zig` (the GT as a v1 file = the embedded model; a synthetic v2 set; every corruption refused:
  truncations, magic, version, counts, flags, CRC, a vertex id, a face's id count, a second shadow face);
  `tools/tests/test_racer_carset.py` (the scale, a fixed colour, a set's header / palette / CRC, refusals);
  `apps/harness/racer_carfile.mjs` + 2 gate lines (a synthetic set as `C:\RACER\CAR.KCR` loads and recolours, a corrupted
  `SALT.KCR` refused on Salt with CAR.KCR drawn instead, a truncated CAR.KCR refused and the GT drawn; `--break crc`).

## The real 32X cars (LOCAL ONLY: never committed, never on a shared card)

`python3 -I tools/racer/vr32x_car.py` (default paths: the dump in `prototypes/racer_re/vr_local/32x/`, git-ignored) writes
`vr32x_car_<class>.kcr` and a report there. Committed: the CODE only (`vr32x_carrom.py` finds the tables by shape,
`vr32x_cargeom.py` the geometry rules, `vr32x_car.py` the sets), tested on a synthetic cartridge
(`tools/tests/test_racer_vr32x_car.py`); no Sega bytes, offsets or values.

- **Found by shape.** The SH-2 copies one block of car models per class to its SDRAM before a race, from a table of chained
  (start, end) ROM addresses; the 68000's per-class lists (the near car, its shadow, the cockpit's wheels) and its corner and
  palette tables confirm three classes and their order. A car model is a course tile's layout plus one rule: a polygon with
  flags bit 5 carries three more words (skipped). The class palette (64 entries over CRAM 32-95) comes from a table that sits
  right before the palettes it points at; entries 1-31 are the grey ramp every course shares.
- **A block:** the near car, its flat shadow, two near variants, the cockpit's wheels and dash, the front and rear axles (two
  wheels each, placed by code), then four AI liveries (a near and a far model and a 2-polygon sprite card each).
- **The three classes** (named by look): `formula` (open wheels), `stock` (a coupe), `prototype` (a closed sports car).
  Ours, per class, a set of 5 models: the player's car = the near model + its two axles at the wheels (found in a far
  model's tyre sides) + the 32X's shadow as our one quad, its far model a box (the player is never far from its camera);
  each AI livery = its near model + the axles, its far model its own.

  | class | player near / far | AI near / far (4 liveries) | colour error (RGB, mean; the player's) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | formula | 143 / 5 faces, 185 vertices | 71-73 / 32-36 | 19.2; 20.5 |
  | stock | 188 / 5, 160 | 73-81 / 16-23 | 13.1; 13.9 |
  | prototype | 158 / 5, 182 | 66-77 / 14-17 | 14.5; 15.0 |

  Over our SOFT budget (64 near faces): taken with `--allow-soft`; the HARD limits hold.
- **Units, axes, winding.** 32 model units = one Q7 step (the inferred factor above); the 32X car is x across, y up, z
  forward, as ours; the winding is checked (most faces facing out of the model), not assumed. A quad that Q7 rounding leaves
  off its plane or non-convex is split in two triangles.
- **Order.** The cart draws a car's faces in the file's order under one key, so the importer sorts them once, farthest
  first from an eye behind and above the car (every chase POV). Seen from the side or the front it can mis-sort.
- **Colours.** The class's colours on the 7 car ramps: ramp 13 (the skidmarks') takes the darkest colour as its level 0, each
  next ramp the base (a used colour, or that colour at full brightness) that cuts the area-weighted error most, the player's
  colours weighted 4x. The F1's red-and-white is red-and-white; an AI livery whose hue lost the vote (the green F1) is drawn
  on its nearest shade.
- **The local card** (`RACER M5 FRONT 32X CARS`): the step 4 32X disk + `CAR.KCR` = the formula set, `DXBAY.KCR` = stock,
  `DXACRO.KCR` = prototype (shots `prototypes/racer_re/vr_local/32x/re/cars/x32b/`).
