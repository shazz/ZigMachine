# Kestrel Racing M3: the race (R82)

Cart 160 `racer`, on top of M2 (`M2.md`). M3 ports freds72's AI cars (MIT; `LICENSE_NOTE.md`) bit for bit, puts the
seven of them on a start grid in four liveries, adds the race rules (checkpoints, laps, time extension, rank, best
lap), a table-driven race FSM, the HUD bands with a minimap, and benchmark **lap v2**, the reference benchmark.
Spec: `prototypes/racer_re/PORT_PLAN.md` section 5, row M3.

## The AI physics bar: bit for bit

`npc.zig` ports `make_pid` and `make_npc`'s control (look 1 to 4 segments ahead for the farthest point of sight, aim, PID
the heading error into the steering angle, brake at 0.12), `contacts.zig` ports `update_contacts` (the player's two
colliders against the first car within 2 units), `car.zig` gained the NPC `steer` (the angle is set, not accumulated;
braking is x0.92), `seg.zig` the `get_next` / `get_length` the AI reads, `race.zig` the original's `_update` order.

**Result: exact.** Against the Lua reference (z8lua + freds72's engine, `tools/racer/ref_harness.py` with the M3 scenarios)
every car's position, velocity, angle, face, track u and v, and the global wall-hit, contact and AI-brake counts match on
every tick, no tolerance, on:

| run | cars | ticks | what it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `race` with 2 AI cars | 3 | 600 | the PID from its first (nil) call, the lookahead on the straight and into the first turn, 98 hard-braking steers |
| `ram`: the player accelerates into AI car 1 parked 6 m ahead | 3 | 300 | `update_contacts`: both colliders, the push and the torque (4 contacts), then the player slides along the outer wall (104 wall-hit ticks) |
| `race` with 7 AI cars (the autopilot drives the player) | 8 | 4,400 | a whole 3-lap race and more, the grid, the crest and the banked turn: a CRC-32 of all 8 cars' state **on every tick**, the finish tick of each car |

A run that could not reach a branch would pass vacuously, so the dump carries coverage columns: `sfx` (the engine's contact
sound: counted per contact) and `nbrake` (NPC hard-braking steers). `race_test.zig` asserts both are non-zero where the
run is meant to exercise them. The 7-car race has **no** contact (the autopilot passes the field clear of it), so the
contacts are pinned by the `ram` run only: say so, do not claim more.

Fixtures (`tools/racer/race_fixture.py`, committed under `apps/zig/assets/screens/racer/`): `race_npc2.bin` (79 KB) and
`race_ram.bin` (40 KB) hold every car's full words every tick; `race_full.bin` (32 KB) holds the per-tick CRC list, the
finish ticks and sparse full rows; `race_v2.btn` (4,400 bytes) is the player's recorded autopilot, the cart's own input
for lap v2 and the headless race. None of freds72's files is committed. `tools/tests/test_racer_race.py` fails if any of
them differs from a fresh z8lua run (skipped without z8lua). `racer_race.mjs` compares the **cart in the browser**
against the same reference: the `racer,cars` line every 60 frames of lap v2 must equal the fixture's CRC at that tick.

`--break pid` (key `W` / `npc.break_pid`: the derivative gain 0.5 -> 0.45): `race_test.zig` and `racer_race.mjs --break pid`
fail against the reference.

### Deliberate differences from the Lua

- **Every car waits for the countdown.** The original's AI cars drive from tick 1 and only the player waits 90 ticks. The
  FSM holds all eight through the grid and the 90-tick countdown; the reference's `RACER_HOLD` does the same (our
  `footer.lua`), so cart race tick n is the reference's row 89 + n.
- **A compact start grid.** The track binary's grid is the player on segment 0 and the seven AI cars two a row on segments
  1 to 4 (the original: eight cars 8 segments, ~30 m, apart). The harness spawns from the same grid, so the reference runs
  are Lua-checkable. The M2 fixtures use slot 0 only and did not move.
- **Only the player feels contacts**, exactly as the original (`plyr:update_contacts(npcs)`): the AI cars pass through each
  other and through the player. Port as is, because anything else breaks the bar; this is the thing to tell Matt (below).
- The NPC's `rpm` is never read back in the original (it passes a literal 0.7 every tick); it is not modelled.
- The AI cars lay no skidmarks (only the player's emitters run); there is no sound.
- **modelView overflow (a latent M2 bug, fixed):** `carview.modelView` added three Q25-by-Q16 products in 32 bits, which
  passes 2^31 once a car is 64 m from the origin along an axis (the oval is 90 m long): wrong placement, a panic in a safe
  build. It now sums in 64 bits; below 64 m the numbers are the same. **M2's lap v1 pins moved:** the last three CRCs (frames 600,
  660 and 720, where the car is past z = 64 m) changed from `569ee5f8 132d82b0 713d6984` to `072c2821 f9536170 9b438a44`, so the
  old ones had the car misplaced; `racer_drive.mjs` and `M2.md` carry the new pins (a board run of M2 must compare to them).

## The race

`rules.zig` (play_state): the clock starts at 75 s; a checkpoint (the track's four, in lap order, now in the binary) adds
10 s and shows "extend time" for 5 s; the fourth closes the lap, the third lap is a win; the clock reaching 0 is a loss,
judged before a checkpoint on the same tick. `standings.zig`: a finisher is ahead of every non-finisher; finishers by
tick; the others by track u; **a tie goes against the player** (the original's `if plyr_u > npc_u then rank - 1`). A car
has finished when its u passes the last checkpoint of the last lap (u > 359 on the 120-segment oval), the same line for all
eight. `records.zig`: best lap and best race of the session, **in RAM only**. **File persistence waits on Q6** (may a cart
write a file): nothing writes a file; `records.zig` is the one place to bind when Q6 is answered. `session.zig` ties it
together; after the player wins or the clock runs out the AI cars run on until all have finished or 20 s have passed.

The FSM (`fsm.zig`) is a table: grid -start-> countdown -go-> race -finish-> finish -done-> results, and `restart`
(Enter / R) from every state but the grid back to the grid. `session_test.zig` takes every (state, event) pair: the 8 legal
ones move to the table's state, the other 17 are refused and leave the state and its tick count alone.

**The pinned 3-lap race** (the recorded driver, `racer_race.mjs`, `session_test.zig`, the same hash in both): the player wins
in **3,726 ticks (2:04.2)**; the AI cars finish at 4,251 4,249 4,242 4,240 4,224 4,218 4,222 (AI cars 1..7, 2:20.6 to
2:21.7); final order `0 6 7 5 4 3 2 1`; CRC of order + finish ticks `ef4ce056`. No car is ever off the asphalt or against a
wall for even one tick while racing (the longest runs are 0; the player's coast after its finish is not counted).
The seven AI cars finish within 1.1 s of each other (33 ticks): the AI has no rubber band.

## Painter's order

Cars, skidmarks and the track share the one bucket sort (M2). M3 adds: eight cars through `carview.draw` (cars beyond R + 1
tiles are not transformed), a per-car livery (`project.Key.dc` shifts the body faces' palette index by the livery's ramp;
ramps 15 to 18 are the cart's, past the track's 15; the player keeps ramp 12), and tests:

- `game_test.zig` **crest**: the player on the near side of the oval's hump, one AI car before the crest and one past it:
  every polygon of the car past the crest sorts before (under) the crest's road face, every polygon of the one before it
  after (over) it. Key `K` stages the pose; `racer_race.mjs` pins its view, and the car past the crest shows 0 pixels while
  the one before it shows 17 (screenshot `race_crest.png`).
- `game_test.zig` **car vs car**: pushed near first and far second, the near car's polygons still come after the far one's.
- **Not solved:** two AI cars that overlap (they can: see contacts) interleave face by face at their own mean depths, and a
  car against a wall is ordered by its floor key (M2) only. A whole-car key would be the next step if the board shows it.

## HUD and minimap

Two text lines in the existing 12-row bands (`racehud.zig`, no plane): the top band is the state's headline (the grid's
prompt, GET READY 3 2 1, `L1/3 0:12.3 B 2:01.0 3RD`, FINISH / TIME OVER and the place, RESULTS with the total time and
RECORD); the bottom band is the speed, the clock, the checkpoint and what to press. The benchmark OSD (`o`) still replaces
the bottom line. The **minimap** (`minimap.zig`) is the racing line seen from above, drawn into the top band's last 28 x 10
pixels (the band is 12 rows high, so the oval is small): one pixel an AI car in its livery, the player 2 x 2 white; a few
hundred byte stores a frame. A map large enough to read would have to cover the view: left to the board test.
`racer_race.mjs` hashes both bands at each FSM state (pinned); `game_test.zig` pins the words.

## Lap v2 (the reference benchmark)

Key `x`: 60 frames on the grid (all 8 cars in view), 90 ticks of countdown, then 1,320 ticks of racing: the player's
first lap and a few seconds more, among the 7 AI cars, **2,880 frames**, then the grid again. It prints what lap v0 and v1
print (the view CRC every 60 frames, the lap summary at the end) and:

```
racer,cars,<race ticks>,<crc32 of all 8 cars' state>,<state>,<place>   every 60 frames (= race_full.bin's CRC at that tick)
racer,state,<state>,<race ticks>                                       on every FSM change
racer,aius,<ticks>,<avg us a tick>,<max us>                            at the round's end: the AI half of a tick (hostMicros; 0 headless)
racer,result,... / racer,watch,...                                     when a race's results show
```

Pinned in `racer_race.mjs`: the 48 view CRCs (hash `16d325b6a608`); the same on the CPU and AFFINE fills; the `racer,cars`
lines equal the Lua reference's CRCs.

## Costs (estimates; no board run yet)

`apps/harness/racer_costs.mjs` (wasm op counts x the fitted cost model, plain multiply twin, oval; `--frames 12 --warmup 20`;
1.67 Mc is a 60 Hz frame at 100 MHz, the board drops below 60 fps between ~1.75 and 2.1 Mc):

| scene | R | CPU fill | AFFINE fill |
|---|---|---|---|
| driving, 1 car (M2) | 5 | 1.56 Mc (cart 0.53), ~60 fps | 1.53 Mc (cart 0.44), ~60 fps |
| grid, 8 cars in view | 5 | 1.87 Mc (cart 0.90), ~53.5 fps | 1.86 Mc (cart 0.81), ~53.7 fps |
| grid, 8 cars in view | 8 | 1.96 Mc (cart 0.97), ~51 fps | 1.95 Mc (cart 0.88), ~51 fps |
| race, mid-lap (lap v2, AI ticking) | 5 | 3.06 Mc (cart 0.97), ~32.7 fps | 2.91 Mc (cart 0.88), ~34.4 fps |
| race, mid-lap (lap v2, AI ticking) | 8 | 3.27 Mc (cart 1.06), ~30.6 fps | 3.12 Mc (cart 0.98), ~32 fps |
| crest pose, 3 cars | 5 | 1.49 Mc (cart 0.57), ~60 fps | 1.48 Mc (cart 0.49), ~60 fps |

- **Seven more cars on screen cost ~0.35 Mc of cart** (0.53 -> 0.90 at the grid): ~0.05 Mc a car at 30 faces and 48 vertices
  each, as the M1 per-vertex model said; the frame goes from 60 to ~53 fps at the grid, the worst view (all 8 cars close).
- **The AI tick** (7 cars: control with its lookahead and PID, physics, ground and wall tests, plus the player's contacts)
  is **~0.2 Mc a tick, ~0.1 Mc a frame at the 30 Hz cadence** (the cart share rises from 0.90 at the grid to 0.97-1.02 mid-race
  with a tick every second frame). All integer: the `i64` ops are the 16.16 multiplies, no float anywhere. `racer,aius` is the
  board's real number.
- **The model's big term is the instruction cache, not the AI's maths.** Mid-lap the code run per frame is ~26 KB against the
  16 KB I$ (lap v1: 22 KB), and the model bills 1.4 Mc of I$ misses (0.65 in lap v1): that is most of the 3.1 Mc and what puts
  lap v2 at ~32 fps in the estimate (lap v1: 2.0 Mc, ~49 fps). It is an op-count model of a footprint, not a measurement; the
  board decides (to-do 2). If it holds, the cut is a code-layout one (tick the AI cars on the frames the renderer is lightest,
  or keep the tick's loops out of the render's cache lines), not fewer cars.
- **So the bar "60 fps in the 7-car race at R = 5" is, by this estimate, not met** (53 fps at the grid, ~33 mid-race); lap v2
  exists to measure exactly that on the board and on PEREGRINE.

## Decisions for Matt

- **Q6 (records to a file)** is still open: records are RAM only; nothing writes a file.
- **Only the player feels car-vs-car contacts**, as in the original, so the AI cars can overlap each other. Making
  contacts two-way would break the bit-for-bit bar (it is a rule change, not a bug); it is cheap to add later behind a flag if
  you want the AI to bump.
- The AI cars wait for the countdown (the original's roll from tick 1): a rule change for the FSM.
- The start grid is compact (player last), so a start shows all eight cars; the original's is a long file of cars.
